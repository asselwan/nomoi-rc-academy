Module 02 · Claim lifecycle fundamentals

Find the first missing checkpoint

A claim can be delayed before it reaches a payer, after the payer receives it, or after the payer decides. The next action depends on which checkpoint has evidence and which one does not.

Follow the evidence, not the status label

A worklist entry marked “pending” is a starting point, not a diagnosis. Ask what event should have happened next, what record would prove it, and who owns the handoff. A prepared claim is not necessarily submitted. A submitted file is not necessarily accepted. An accepted claim is not necessarily paid. A remittance showing payment is not proof that cash was posted and reconciled.

This is an operational tracing method, not a payer rule or a promise of recovery. Check the current payer contract and submission guidance before acting on any real claim. Do not infer a write-off from a missing status or a denied line alone.

Six checkpoints to trace

01 · Front end

Registration and coverage

Confirm the encounter and coverage details were captured for the service date. Evidence: registration record and dated eligibility response. If absent: route to the registration or eligibility owner before assuming a billing failure.

02 · Preparation

Documentation, coding, and charge review

Confirm the billable services have supporting documentation and passed the team's coding and charge checks. Evidence: completed review or a specific hold reason. If held: send the exact gap to the documentation or coding owner.

03 · Outbound

Submission and technical acknowledgement

Find the outbound batch record, then the receiving gateway or payer acknowledgement. Evidence: transmission identifier and an accepted or rejected response. If there is no acknowledgement: investigate transmission or validation before calling it a payer denial.

04 · Payer decision

Adjudication and remittance

For an accepted submission, find the payer's decision in the remittance and distinguish paid, denied, and still undecided lines. Evidence: remittance tied to the submitted claim or line. If absent: follow up on the payer response; do not invent an outcome.

05 · Recovery

Denial review or correction

For a denied line, verify its reason and supporting records, then choose a documented correction, appeal, or closure path under the current payer terms. Evidence: reason, owner, action, and next-review date. A denial by itself does not establish that an appeal will succeed.

06 · Financial close

Posting and reconciliation

Match the remittance to posted payment or adjustment and investigate any difference. Evidence: posted transaction and reconciliation record. A paid remittance with no matching posting is a reconciliation task, not a new claim submission.
